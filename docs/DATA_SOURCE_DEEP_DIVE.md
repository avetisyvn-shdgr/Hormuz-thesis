## 1. Project summary (ten lines)

1. **Objective.** Causal-inference Bachelor thesis (TUM, Transportation Analytics) on the 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption and LNG freight markets, testing a "ton-mile multiplier" mechanism; operational-onset cutoff locked at `2026-02-28`.
2. **Original dependent variable.** Spark30S (Atlantic, Sabine Pass→Gate, 174k m³ 2-stroke) and Spark25S (Pacific, NWS→Tianjin) daily spot LNG freight assessments in USD/day.
3. **Original ton-mile mechanism.** `ais_laden_tonmiles_usgc` — AIS-derived **laden** ton-miles to the US Gulf Coast replacement origin; pre-committed in H3 as the contribution-bearing test.
4. **Current free-data outcome.** No freight-rate outcome at all. The locked working primary is IMF PortWatch daily Hormuz/Panama tanker transit counts and deadweight capacity, with a WTO/AXSMarine LNG-only outbound **index** as an LNG-specific robustness outcome.
5. **Current capacity-distance proxy.** GFW terminal-sequence voyages × nominal vessel capacity (m³) × **modelled** route distance = "inferred capacity-nautical miles" (m³-nm), plus speed-assumption modelled vessel-days.
6. **Result standing.** +10.20% mean capacity-distance per retained voyage (BCa 95% CI +4.39% to +17.05%), but Kitagawa/Oaxaca decomposition attributes most of it to route-share composition, not to any route becoming longer.
7. **Remaining price-data gap.** There is still **no verified free historical daily freight series** for Y_t; Spark premium is paywalled, the non-authenticated Spark endpoint is latest-value/N-4 only, and the ICE-futures "proxy" was demoted on 2026-06-14 as having no confirmed free EOD feed.
8. **Remaining vessel/cargo-data gap.** No observed cargo mass, no laden/ballast flag, no observed sailed distance, no voyage identifier, no waiting/idling time — hence the standing prohibition on calling the current measure observed cargo ton-miles.
9. **Governance constraints.** `registry.get_variable()` is the only admitted ingestion path; `config/sources.yaml` status flags are binding; training must remain strictly pre-`2026-02-28` with rolling-origin validation.
10. **Consequence for this scan.** Anything bought or granted must slot into the registry as a *documented* swap, must reach back at least to 2022-01-01 (ideally 2019), and must permit publication of derived coefficients.

## 2. What would actually close each gap

| Gap | Minimum acceptable data | Ideal data |
|---|---|---|
| Y_t (price) | One consistently-defined LNG spot carrier series in USD/day, ≥52 weekly pre-onset observations, covering 2026-02-28 | Daily Atlantic **and** Pacific spot day rates for 174k m³ 2-stroke, plus ballast rates, from ≤2019 |
| X_t (mechanism) | Voyage-level records with IMO, load/discharge terminal, departure/arrival timestamps and laden flag | Add actual loaded cargo volume (m³ or MMBtu/tonnes) and observed sailed distance from AIS track, plus canal/waiting time |
| Controls | JKM/TTF/HH daily, LNG bunker price, canal congestion, liquefaction outages | Add route-level arb spreads and fleet availability |

## 6. Circularity and methodological warnings carried forward

1. **Platts Global LNG route costs must not be used as evidence that distance causes price.** The series is constructed from day rates, ballast rates and an assumed route duration; regressing price on distance using it is mechanically self-fulfilling.
2. **ICE/CME settlements are not physical spot assessments.** Roll, term structure and liquidity differ; the Spark30S future is *settled on* the Spark30S assessment, so it is a lagged average, not the daily print.
3. **AXSMarine is now Signal Ocean.** If Signal Ocean voyage data enter the panel, the WTO Hormuz LNG index stops being an independent validator.
4. **Kpler is a Spark shareholder.** Using Kpler flows to explain Spark prices is not a clean instrument.
5. **Fearnleys rates are broker estimates**, not necessarily transacted fixtures — this must be stated wherever they are used.
6. **Platts carrier day rates are assessed on a TFDE basis**, while the thesis specification and Fearnleys/Baltic-174 series are 2-stroke. Mixing the two changes the level and possibly the volatility of Y_t.
7. **PortWatch tanker aggregates are not LNG-specific flows**; the existing `n_tanker` choice is already documented as a diluted proxy.
8. **A before/after percentage is not a training set.** Any model must train strictly before 2026-02-28 with chronological rolling-origin validation; post-onset observations are for evaluation only.
9. **Obtaining better data improves measurement, not identification.** None of the sources below turns the design into a causal estimate on its own.

### B. Free-data implementation shortlist (five, usable immediately)

1. **IMF PortWatch** — keep as the locked working primary; already integrated and re-verified live today.
2. **NOAA/BOEM MarineCadastre AIS** — the highest-value *new* free addition. It yields observed AIS positions and precise departure/arrival timestamps for LNG carriers at every US Gulf liquefaction terminal, which converts the US-side leg of the replacement corridor from modelled to observed. Confirm 2026 coverage before committing.
3. **GIE ALSI** — daily, LNG-specific European terminal send-out and inventory. Gives an independent discharge-side series to validate the inferred European arrivals in the capacity-nautical-mile reconstruction.
4. **WTO/AXSMarine Hormuz LNG index** — keep as the LNG-specific robustness outcome, with the independence caveat now documented.
5. **EIA Open Data** — keep for Henry Hub and Brent controls; add the monthly US LNG export series by destination as a coarse cross-check on the reconstructed flows.

ENTSOG, Eurostat, e-Stat and ACCC remain useful but secondary; GFW stays as-is.

### C. Three minimum viable combinations

**1. Best fully open-data design.**
PortWatch (primary) + WTO LNG index + MarineCadastre AIS + GIE ALSI + GFW + EIA/Eurostat/e-Stat.
*Can identify:* the operational throughput disruption at Hormuz; an LNG-specific outbound volume change; **observed** US-Gulf-side departure timing and in-EEZ track; European discharge-side response; importer-origin reallocation.
*Cannot identify:* any freight-price effect whatsoever; actual cargo mass; full ocean sailed distance; laden vs ballast state with confidence. The ton-mile construct stays `capacity × modelled distance` outside US waters.

**2. Conditional academic-access design.**
Combination 1 **plus** Clarksons SIN (library) and/or Fearnleys weekly MEGI/XDF, plus a Spire academic AIS extract.
*Could add, only if access is actually granted:* a weekly LNG day-rate outcome around 2026-02-28 with a genuine pre-period; observed sailed distance and voyage duration for the roster; a fleet-availability control.
*Cannot identify:* the pre-committed Spark30S/25S daily route-specific estimand; actual loaded cargo volume. Basin-level rates cannot test route-specific pricing.

**3. Best-case publishable design with proprietary access.**
Spark30S/25S daily (Y_t) + Baltic BLNG-174 and Argus as independent measurement replicates + Kpler voyage/cargo records (X_t) + Spire or MarineCadastre observed tracks + Platts ballast rates and JKM/TTF/HH controls.
*Can identify:* a route-matched daily price outcome; an X_t that is genuinely `actual cargo mass × observed sailed distance`; measurement error in Y_t via multiple assessors; a defensible mechanism decomposition.
*Cannot identify:* causality by itself. With one treated event, one date and a small post-period, this remains an event study with counterfactual triangulation. Better data raise construct validity and the contribution; they do not deliver identification, and the thesis must keep saying so.

### E. Decision statement

- **Best single source for the freight-price outcome:** **Spark Commodities (Spark30S/Spark25S)**. It is the pre-committed estimand, it is route-matched to the thesis, it is daily, and it is explicitly available on a `174-2stroke` basis. Nothing else removes the substitution problem.
- **Best single source for the ton-mile mechanism:** **Kpler**, because it is the only entity found whose LNG product carries cargo volume, vessel identity, terminals and voyage geometry in one schema. Spire is the better source if the priority is *observed sailed distance* specifically rather than cargo mass.
- **Unverified acquisition hypotheses:** **Clarksons Research SIN via a possible
  university-library entitlement** and **Fearnleys weekly MEGI/XDF via a direct
  academic request**. Neither TUM entitlement nor Fearnleys historical-file
  delivery, cost, or reuse rights has been established.
- **Strongest free combination:** **PortWatch + MarineCadastre AIS + GIE ALSI + WTO LNG index + EIA.** MarineCadastre and ALSI are the two genuinely new additions and they attack the mechanism's weakest link — modelled distance and unobserved arrivals — from both ends of the US-Gulf-to-Europe corridor.
- **Highest expected-value next action:** **Check whether TUM already licenses
  Clarksons SIN, LSEG Workspace, or a Bloomberg terminal, and separately request
  Fearnleys and Baltic Exchange access terms.** The enquiries themselves are
  low-cost; usable data, historical coverage, price, and publication rights
  remain unknown. Verifying MarineCadastre's 2026 coverage is the best free-side
  action in the same session.
